Teams often ask whether a **product page optimizer** replaces a **technical site crawl**. It does not — they solve different layers of SEO.

## Site-wide crawls (Screaming Frog, Semrush, Ahrefs)

**Best for:**

- Finding broken links, redirect chains, and 404s across the domain  
- Auditing robots.txt, sitemap coverage, and crawl budget issues  
- Template-level problems (duplicate titles across 10,000 SKUs)  
- Backlink and rank tracking  

**Limitation for PDPs:** Crawlers excel at **scale**, not at telling you the exact copy-paste fix for one hero product’s meta description or Product schema.

## Single-URL product page optimizers (Utiliy)

**Best for:**

- Launching or refreshing **one high-intent PDP**  
- Scoring Google SEO, schema, keywords, images, and AI visibility together  
- Generating **ready-to-paste fixes** in an AI lab  
- Benchmarking a competitor’s product listing  

**Limitation:** Not a replacement for monitoring your entire catalog weekly.

## Recommended workflow

```
Site crawl (quarterly)  →  fix template & technical issues
        ↓
Utiliy per hero SKU     →  fix titles, schema, images, AI gaps
        ↓
Content tools (optional)→  long-form blog/support content
```

## When Utiliy complements Screaming Frog

| Scenario | Tool |
|----------|------|
| “500 URLs return soft 404” | Site crawl |
| “This launch SKU needs perfect schema + meta today” | Utiliy |
| “Duplicate titles on collection templates” | Site crawl |
| “Why doesn’t AI recommend our product?” | Utiliy AI visibility pillar |
| “Blog post keyword optimization” | Surfer / Jasper |

## Does Utiliy crawl my whole store?

No. You paste **one product URL** at a time. That is intentional — depth over breadth for the page that must rank and convert.
