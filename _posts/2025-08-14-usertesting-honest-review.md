UserTesting isn't new. I joined in March 2024, ignored it for two months, then kept the dashboard open while working remote in July 2025. Total payout: **$127.00** via PayPal for roughly **6 hours 20 minutes** of recorded tests.

That's **~$20/hour** — better than surveys, worse than billable network consulting.

## How a test actually works

1. Notification email or dashboard badge: "New test available"
2. You have **minutes** to start before it's gone
3. Screener questions — age, job, software you use
4. If accepted: screen + mic recording, talk-aloud tasks ("Find a hotel under $150/night")
5. Upload, wait for review, payment in ~7 days

Average test length for me: **12–18 minutes**. Longest: 45-minute live interview ($60).

**Screen-out rate:** 4 disqualifications I started but didn't finish payout for, out of 15 attempts (~27%).

## What increases your odds

- **Profile completeness:** Added LinkedIn, listed dev tools I actually use
- **Quiet room:** Background AC noise got one test rejected — re-recorded
- **Fast clickers lose:** They want narration, not speedrunning

## Regional reality check

I'm in Oregon, US. Test volume varies wildly by country. A friend in Croatia sees **one test per week**. Don't extrapolate my numbers globally.

## Taxes (US)

UserTesting sends 1099-NEC if you cross thresholds. I track payouts in a spreadsheet — date, amount, minutes. Boring, necessary.

## Who should use it

- Remote workers who can pause for 15 minutes
- People comfortable thinking out loud
- Anyone targeting **$100–300/month**, not rent money

## Who should skip it

- People needing predictable hours
- Anyone uncomfortable being recorded
- Night-shift workers — tests peak US business hours

## Alternatives I'm testing for a follow-up

- **TryMyUI** — lower volume, similar format
- **Respondent** — higher pay, harder qualification

I'll publish a three-way comparison after 30 days of parallel use.
